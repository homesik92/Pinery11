Approval and exceptions

Do I need approval before I change something outside my home or lot?

Usually, yes. The Declaration requires written approval from the Design Review Committee before improvements are started, and the guidelines say anything not listed in them also needs approval. Many everyday jobs are listed as not needing approval when you follow the rules for that item, for example repainting in the same colors or replacing landscaping plants.

In the guidelines: 1.1 Basis for Guidelines 2.1 General 3.1 General Open PDF page 5

What can I do without asking?

Examples the guidelines say need no approval when the stated conditions are met: re-staining or repainting in substantially the same colors, replacing or adding plants that keep the lot's look or save water, repairing a driveway without expanding it, flower gardens, vegetable gardens in a side or rear yard, satellite dishes up to two and a half feet across, garage-mounted basketball backboards, solar LED lights, replacing a mailbox, holiday decorations that come down promptly, and matching screen doors or shutters.

How do I ask for approval?

Send the Committee a drawing or plan before work starts. It should be drawn to a scale of 1/4 inch to 1 foot, show your lot lines and the proposed improvement, describe the materials and colors, and include your name, address and phone number. Submit two copies with the $10 review fee. Plans sent without the fee are returned unapproved.

In the guidelines: 3.2 Drawings or Plans 3.3 Submission of Drawings and Plans 3.4 Review Fee Open PDF page 28

Where do I send my request?

The 2016 guidelines give the Committee's address as the Master Association's, 6950 Pinery Parkway South, Parker, Colorado 80134. That is the address as printed in 2016, so check with the board for the current one.

In the guidelines: 1.4 Committee Address and Phone 3.3 Submission of Drawings and Plans Open PDF page 6

How long does the Committee have to answer?

Forty-five days after it has received everything it asked for. If the Committee does not send a disapproval or a request for more information within those 45 days, the request is treated as approved.

In the guidelines: 3.5 Action by the Committee 3.6 Failure of Committee to Act on Plans Open PDF page 29

After approval, how long do I have to build?

Work must begin within 180 days of approval or the approval becomes void and you must reapply. Work must be finished within nine months of approval unless the Committee extends the time.

In the guidelines: 3.7 Prosecution of Work Open PDF page 30

What if my request is denied?

You can appeal to the Board of Directors by written notice within 30 days of the denial, conditions or refusal. After you have used the appeal steps, you may take the decision to a court of law.

In the guidelines: 3.9 Rights of Appeal 3.10 Appeal to Court of Law Open PDF page 30

Does approval mean my project meets code and is safe?

No. Approval covers appearance only. You are still responsible for Douglas County permits and building codes, for locating utility lines and easements, and for any damage you cause to them. If the county's rules and these guidelines differ, the more restrictive rule applies.

In the guidelines: 3.11 Nonliability 1.7 Effect of Governmental and Other Regulations 1.8 Interference with Utilities 2.25 County Regulations Open PDF page 31

Who is on the Design Review Committee?

Three members appointed by the Master Association's Board of Directors.

In the guidelines: 1.3 Design Review Committee Open PDF page 6

I bought my home and something on the lot does not meet the guidelines. Do I have to remove it?

Not necessarily. If an earlier owner's improvement was approved in advance, or a previous owner's improvement is nonconforming, the current owner may not have to remove it or be fined, unless at the time of purchase you knew or should have known about it, for example because of a lien or pending suit.

In the guidelines: 2.62 Nonconforming Improvements Open PDF page 20

What if the guidelines and the Declaration disagree?

The Declaration controls. If a question comes up about what a term or rule means, the Committee's interpretation is final. The board and Committee may also add to or change the guidelines, so confirm you have the latest edition before you start a project.

Everyday rules

Where can I park?

No vehicle may be parked regularly, meaning more than three days a week, on a public street in the community. Regular parking belongs in a garage or driveway. Boats, campers, trailers, motor homes, trucks other than pickups and other large vehicles must be kept in a structure designed for them, except guests' vehicles for up to four days. The Association can have a vehicle that sits on the street for more than five days removed at the owner's expense.

In the guidelines: 2.67 Parking 2.23 Commercial Vehicles 2.61 Motor Homes 2.52 Junk Vehicles Open PDF page 21

How many pets can I have, and what are the rules?

Only standard household pets are allowed. No more than two adult pets that mostly use outdoor areas for exercise or waste. Pets may not run at large, and habitually barking, howling or yelping dogs count as a nuisance. Livestock, poultry, rabbits, snakes and exotic animals are prohibited.

In the guidelines: 2.71 Pets 2.7 Animals Open PDF page 22

When can I put my trash out?

Not more than 24 hours before the scheduled pickup. Garbage cans must otherwise be screened from view of neighbors and the street, and trash may not be allowed to accumulate on a lot.

In the guidelines: 2.102 Trash Containers and Enclosures Open PDF page 26

Can I hang laundry outside?

Temporary clotheslines or hangers in a back or side yard are acceptable if they are not left up for multiple days in a row.

In the guidelines: 2.21 Clothes Lines and Hangers Open PDF page 11

Where can I store firewood?

In the side or back yard next to the house, neatly stacked, out of the way of drainage, and screened by planting or matching building materials so it cannot be seen from neighbors, trails or streets.

What are the rules for outdoor lighting?

Maintaining or replacing existing fixtures needs no approval, and commercially available solar LED lighting of similar brightness is fine. Floodlights and motion-activated security lights need approval. Place lights so they do not shine into neighboring lots.

Can I put up a sign?

For sale and for rent signs larger than six square feet are prohibited, as are billboards and poster boards or advertising structures of any kind.

Can I run a business from my home?

Lots are for residential use only. No trade, business or commercial activity is allowed except a permitted business activity described in the Declaration, and nothing may become a nuisance.

How must I keep up my yard?

Each owner must keep the lot in first-class condition, including pruning, cutting ground cover and weeding. Removing dead trees, branches and plants is routine maintenance and needs no approval. Replacing plants with ones that keep the lot's look or reduce watering, including xeriscaping, also needs no approval, but major re-landscaping that significantly changes the lot's appearance does.

What if my home is damaged?

Start repairs within 30 days, which includes contacting your insurer and contractors. A damaged shed or outbuilding that cannot be repaired must be removed within 60 days. If a home is uninhabitable or a total loss, notify the Master Association board.

Building and changes

What do I need to know before building a fence?

Boundary fences may not exceed five feet and must let light pass through. Privacy fences need approval. Tell adjacent neighbors before replacing a fence. For a new boundary fence where none existed, talk to the neighbors first. If they do not object, no approval is needed, and if they do, the board or Committee decides. Plastic, chicken wire, hog wire, barbed wire, electric, chain link, wire mesh, slump block, concrete block and strand wire fences are not allowed along lot boundaries.

In the guidelines: 2.36 Fences Open PDF page 14

Can I add a shed or other outbuilding?

It needs approval. Unless the guidelines say otherwise, the maximum size is 8 by 10 feet and 8 1/2 feet high. It must look like the house, be screened by a fence or plantings, and not unreasonably block a neighbor's view of the mountains or open areas.

In the guidelines: 2.2 Accessory Buildings and Outbuildings 2.3 Additions and Expansions Open PDF page 8

How far from the property lines must I build?

For a house, 20 feet from the front, 7.5 feet at the sides and 20 feet at the rear. For an accessory building, 20 feet front, 10 feet at the sides and 10 feet at the rear. The county's rules also apply, and the stricter one controls.

In the guidelines: 2.15 Building Setbacks 2.25 County Regulations Open PDF page 10

Can I change my siding or exterior colors?

Re-staining or repainting in substantially the same colors needs no approval. The guidelines also allow the earth-tone colors on the sample board they refer to. Metal or vinyl siding and exposed mill-finish aluminum or galvanized flashing are not permitted. Newer wood-look materials need approval, and you may be asked for samples.

In the guidelines: 2.66 Painting 2.35 Exterior Surfaces Open PDF page 20

Can I build or replace a deck, patio or patio cover?

A new deck needs approval. Replacing a deck that does not substantially change the old one, and routine repair, does not. Open patios and patio covers need approval, and for a new one you should talk to neighbors who can see it and share their feedback with the Committee. Enclosing a patio is an addition and needs detailed plans.

What about pools, hot tubs and play equipment?

Pools need approval and must be landscaped and screened, and above-ground pools are discouraged. Hot tubs go in the side or rear yard as part of a deck or patio and must not be immediately visible to neighbors, streets or trails. Swing sets, play houses, skateboard ramps and sandboxes need approval and must be in a fenced rear yard. Treehouses are not permitted.

In the guidelines: 2.75 Pools 2.48 Hot Tubs 2.73 Play and Sports Equipment 2.104 Treehouses Open PDF page 23

Can I install a satellite dish or antenna?

A dish from a commercial satellite provider such as DirecTV, DISH or ViaSat needs no approval if it is no more than two and a half feet across. Shortwave, large or unsightly antennae are not allowed, and the board or Committee decides what counts as large or unsightly. Wind-powered electric generators are not permitted.

In the guidelines: 2.8 Antennae Open PDF page 9

Can I install solar panels or an air conditioner?

The guidelines say heating and cooling units may not sit on a roof, but they except solar panels. They do not give a separate approval process for solar, and rooftop equipment generally needs approval, so ask the Committee first. Replacing an air conditioner in the same spot needs no approval. A new location should be chosen to limit visibility and noise for neighbors, and roof-mounted air conditioning is prohibited.

Does anything affect drainage or utilities?

Changes that significantly affect drainage need approval, and water must be able to flow freely across lots and away from foundations. You maintain the drainage swales and driveway culverts on your lot. All utility lines must be underground, and underground installations need approval.

What is never allowed?

Among the things the guidelines prohibit outright: metal or vinyl siding, treehouses, wells, individual water supplies and sewage disposal systems, above-ground storage tanks, oil and gas drilling or mining, dirt or gravel driveways, wind-powered generators, livestock and exotic animals, chain link and similar boundary fences, reflective materials in windows, roof-mounted air conditioning, and discharging firearms except in self-defense.

Article I. Introduction

1.1 Basis for Guidelines

These Architectural Guidelines and Rules and Regulations are intended to assist the Owners in The Pinery Filing No. 11 in maintaining the overall general appearance and “feel” of the neighborhood as well as preserving property values by providing the Master Association a mechanism for recourse should an Owner of The Pinery Filing No. 11 implement an “improvement” considered by a majority of Members to be detrimental to the surrounding property values. As provided for in Section 4.14 of the Declaration of Protective Covenants and Restrictions of The Pinery Filing No. 11 ("Declaration"), these rules and regulations are adopted by the Master Association with respect to the use of Lots. The DECLARATION REQUIRES PRIOR APPROVAL OF THE DESIGN REVIEW COMMITTEE BEFORE ANY "IMPROVEMENTS" ARE COMMENCED ON OR TO A LOT IN THE PINERY FILING NO. 11. As of 2016, all of the lots in Filing No 11 have been built. These Architectural Guidelines were rewritten to significantly reduce the oversight required of the Design Review Committee and Master Association for ongoing maintenance and upkeep of properties and structures within the Pinery Filing NO. 11.

Previously, plans and supporting documentation were required to be submitted to the Design Review Committee prior to the commencement of any Improvements pursuant to Article III of these Guidelines. Section 6.2 of the Declaration provides that no structure, including but not limited to residences, accessory buildings, antennae, flag poles, fences, walls, house numbers, mail boxes, exterior lighting, or other improvements shall be constructed or maintained upon any Lot, nor shall any alteration, nor shall any replat of any Lots, nor shall any repainting to the exterior of a surface, be made, nor shall any landscaping be performed (all of the foregoing being referred to herein as "Improvements") without the prior written approval of the Committee and Senior Corp., the Declarant under the Declaration.

To assist Lot Owners, the Declaration authorizes the Master Association and Design Review Committee to establish guidelines for approval, and to adopt rules and regulations. As of 2016, this booklet contains the guidelines and rules and regulations established by the Master Association. Throughout this document the term "property" shall refer to a Lot, and the term "Committee" shall refer to the Design Review Committee. All undefined terms shall have the meanings set forth in the Declaration.

1.9 Goal of Guidelines

Compliance with the Guidelines and Rules and Regulations, the provisions of the Declaration will help preserve the inherent architectural and aesthetic quality of The Pinery Filing No. 11. It is important that the Improvements to property be made in harmony with and not detrimental to the rest of the community. A spirit of cooperation with the Committee and neighbors will go far in creating an optimum environment which will benefit all Owners. By following these Guidelines and Rules and Regulations and obtaining approvals for Improvements to property from the Committee, Owners will be protecting their financial investment and will help insure that improvements to property are compatible with standards established for The Pinery Filing No. 11. If a question ever arises as to the correct interpretation of any terms, phrases or language contained in these Guidelines and Rules and Regulations, the Committee's interpretation thereof shall be final and binding.

1.10 Residential Purposes Only

It is expressly agreed that all Lots shall be for residential purposes only and no building shall be erected or placed on any Lot other than one private residential structure, together with a private garage and such outbuildings as are customarily appurtenant to such a dwelling. No more than one single family, its servants and occasional guests may reside upon any one Lot. 2.2 Accessory Buildings and Outbuildings

Approval is required to construct or install an accessory or outbuilding. Approval will be based upon, but not limited to, the following criteria:

  1. (a) Must be of design similar and complementary to the design of the residence, and materials similar or identical with the residence.
  2. (b) Maximum sizes for Accessory Buildings and Outbuildings not specifically referred to in these Guidelines will be 8' x 10' x 8-1/2' height including skids, foundation or concrete slab. Requests for approval will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis, taking into consideration the Lot size, square footage of the residence and proposed location of Accessory Building or Outbuilding.
  3. (c) Must be screened by a fence, or shrubs and/or trees equal to 3/4 the height of the shed or accessory building at planting.
  4. (d) Highest point must not be more than 4' above the top of the screening fence at its lowest point.
  5. (e) Roof should be complementary to existing roof on residence.
  6. (f) Should not unreasonably obstruct adjacent neighbor's view of mountains or open areas.

2.26 Damage

In the event of damage to any residence or other structure located upon a Lot, repairs shall be initiated within thirty (30) days. Initiation of repairs includes coordination with the Owner’s insurance organization and contracting companies in order to affect the repair to restore the damaged residence or structure to its original state. Should an Accessory Building or Outbuilding be damaged beyond repair, the structure and debris shall be removed from the Lot within sixty (60) days.

Should a residence be rendered uninhabitable and/or a total loss, the Master Association board of directors should be notified such that they may determine appropriate Master Association action.

2.54 Landscaping

As The Pinery Filing No. 11 was being developed, initial occupancy of a Lot required compliance to landscaping guidelines intended to produce a consistent environment and neighborhood feel with the intention of associated individual property valuation over time.

Those initial landscaping guidelines/regulations of The Pinery Filing No. 11 successfully produced a reasonably consistent appearance and “feel” for the neighborhood. As stated at the beginning of this document, as of 2016 the intent of these Guidelines are to maintain the overall general appearance and feel of the neighborhood as well as to preserve property values.

As of 2016, the 45 Lots associated with the Master Association have all been built and landscaping has been established. Accordingly, the original 1987 methodology of computing requirements for the minimum numbers of deciduous and evergreen trees based lot square footage did not seem to be a viable methodology to determine ongoing landscaping maintenance requirements for the Pinery Filing No. 11. Additionally, given the duration of time since their original instantiation and the turnover of individual Lot ownership, utilization of originally approved landscaping schemes for each Lot as a mechanism for dictating the replacement and maintenance of originally existing shrubs and lawns did not seem to be viable.

With drought, watering restriction considerations, and a general increased awareness of water conservation issues, the replacement over time of high water usage landscaping with xeriscaping schemes utilizing drought tolerant plants, shrubs, and grasses is anticipated to increase throughout the Pinery Filing No. 11 with the intent of reducing or eliminating the need for supplemental watering from irrigation.

Therefore, the addition of new, or replacement of existing landscaping plants, trees, shrubs, and grasses with materials that generally preserve the current appearance of the Lot or are intended to reduce the need for supplemental watering do not require Master Association or Committee approval.

Article III. Procedure For Committee Approval

3.1 General

As indicated in the listing of specific types of Improvements, there are some cases in which advance written approval of the Master Association or the Committee is not required if the guidelines with respect to that specific type of Improvement are followed. In a few cases, as indicated in the listing, a specific type of Improvement is not permitted under any circumstances. In all other cases, including Improvements not include in these guidelines, Master Association or Committee approval is required before an Improvement to a property is commenced. This section of these guidelines explains how such approval may be requested.

PDF page 28 · FAQ · Top

3.2 Drawings or Plans

Prior to commencement of work on any Improvement to property, the Owner must submit to the Master Association or Committee sufficient information to allow the Master Association or Committee to arrive at a determination of the acceptability of the proposed Improvement. Depending on the proposed Improvement, this information may include descriptions, surveys, plot plans, drainage plans, elevation drawings, constructions plans, specifications and samples of materials and colors as the Committee may reasonably request showing the nature, kind, shape, exterior design, height, width, color, building materials and location of the proposed Improvement plotted horizontally and vertically, the location and size of driveways, the general plan of landscaping, fencing, walls and windbreaks, and the grading plan as applicable. In some cases, the materials to be submitted will not have to be professionally prepared by an architect, and landscape architect or draftsman and a simple drawing and description will be sufficient. In the case of major Improvements, such as original construction of a residence, room additions, structural changes or Outbuilding construction, detailed plans and specifications, prepared by a licensed architect, may be required. Whether done by the Owner or professionally, the following guidelines should be followed in preparing drawings or plans.

  1. (A) The drawing or plan should be done to a scale of 1/4" = 1' and should depict the property lines of the Lot and the outside boundary lines of the residence and Outbuildings as located on the Lot, if constructed. If the Owner has a copy of any improvement survey of the Lot obtained when the residence was purchased, this survey would be an excellent base from which to draw.
  2. (B) The proposed Improvements should be shown on the plan and labelled. Either on the plan or on an attachment, there should be a brief description of the proposed Improvement, including the materials to be used and the colors (Example: Redwood deck, 10 feet by 12 feet with two inch by four inch decking. Natural stain.)
  3. (C) The plan or drawing and other materials should show the name of the Lot Owner, the address and a telephone number where the Lot Owner can be reached.

Two copies of the drawing or plan should be submitted to the Committee at the address stated in the introductory part of these Guidelines One copy will be returned to the Owner after the Committee has acted, showing the Committee's decision. The other copies will be kept for the Committee's records.

3.4 Review Fee

Section 6.3 of the Declaration authorizes the Committee to set and collect a fee for the review plans on proposed Improvements. A ten dollar ($10.00) fee shall be collected from the Owner requesting approval from the Master Association or the Committee prior to review of any such plans. If the fee is not paid with the Plans submitted, the Plans shall be returned unapproved to the Owner. (At the Committee's discretion, upon cancellation the entire fee may be refunded.)

3.5 Action by the Committee

The Committee will meet regularly to review all plans submitted for approval. The Committee may require submission of additional material and may postpone action until all required materials have been submitted. The Committee will contact the Owner or Owner's representative, by phone if possible, if they feel additional materials are necessary or if they need additional information or have any suggestions for change. A quorum at any meeting of the Committee shall consist of two (2) members and any decision shall be reached by a majority of such members. Under Section 6.3 of the Declaration, the Committee must act on the plans within forty-five (45) days after receipt of all materials required by the Committee (unless the time is extended by mutual agreement). The decisions of the Committee shall be promptly transmitted to the persons or entity submitting the request for approval (the "Applicant") at the address of the Applicant furnished by the Applicant to the Committee.

3.6 Failure of Committee to Act on Plans

Any request for approval shall be deemed approved by the Committee, unless notice of disapproval of a request for additional information or materials is given to the Applicant by the Committee within forty-five (45) days after the date of receipt by the Committee of all required materials, unless such time period is extended by mutual agreement.

3.7 Prosecution of Work

  1. (a) Construction of Improvements approved by the Committee must commence within a period of one hundred eighty (180) days from the date of approval, unless the Committee provide otherwise. If no construction activity has taken place within such period, then any approvals of the Committee shall be considered null and void, and any later commencement of construction or alteration that was previously approved shall not be so commenced until a new application, including the payment of necessary fees, has been submitted to and final approval issued by the Committee.
  2. (b) All construction or alteration must be completed within nine (9) months from the date the construction or alteration is approved; provided, however, that the time limit for completion of the construction or alteration may be extended by the Committee as deemed appropriate.

3.9 Rights of Appeal

An Owner may, under Sections 6.6 of the Declaration, appeal to the Board of Directors of The Pinery Filing No. 11 in the event of adverse action by the Committee The Applicant may appeal to the Board by giving written notice of such appeal to the Board of Directors, the Committee within thirty (30) days after the denial, imposition of conditions or refusal of approval. The Board shall appoint a tribunal pursuant to the Bylaws of the Master Association and the tribunal shall hear, de novo, the appeal in accordance with the provisions of the Bylaws for notice and hearing, and, based upon the tribunal's findings and recommendations, the Board shall be approved, disapproved or modified.

3.10 Appeal to Court of Law

Nothing herein shall preclude the right of an aggrieved Applicant to appeal any decision of the Committee, or the Master Association board of directors, to a court of law for a final decision which shall be based on the records of the Committee or the Master Association board of directors; provided, however, such aggrieved Applicant must first have exhausted all of his or her remedies and appeal procedures as are authorized under these Guidelines, and the Declaration.

3.11 Nonliability

The Master Association and/or the Committee, or their duly authorized representatives shall not be liable, in any manner, for any action or failure of action taken hereunder. The Master Association and/or the Committee shall exercise their best judgement to see that all Improvements, construction, landscaping and alterations of lands and structures within The Pinery Filing No. 11 conform to and harmonize with existing surroundings and structures. Approvals of the Master Association and/or the Committee are related solely to the aesthetics and no party shall rely upon such approvals as certifying structural integrity, safety, engineering, soil conditions or absence of natural hazards; and the Master Association and/or the Committee shall not be liable for defects in the structural integrity, engineering or soils conditions or from effects of natural hazards.
